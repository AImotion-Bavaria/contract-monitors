\begin{abstract}
\emph{Background:} Components based on machine learning cannot be verified exhaustively at design time; in safety-critical architectures, they are therefore safeguarded by runtime monitors. How the monitors of the components relate to the requirements of the system, and whether the monitors themselves are correct, usually remains an informal argument. \emph{Aim:} This work derives monitors and their placement from hierarchically refined assume/guarantee contracts such that the monitors of the components provably safeguard the contract of the system. \emph{Method:} We define a contract framework over timed streams, a contract language decidable on finite observations with bounded memory, and a monitor synthesis. All results are mechanised in the proof assistant Lean~4, and the verified monitors are executed directly. \emph{Results:} The synthesised monitors are proven correct.
