\paragraph{Evaluation Planning.} We evaluate the approach on the perception pipeline of Autoware~\cite{Kato2018}, an open-source software stack for automated driving built on ROS~2. The system under consideration receives point clouds and the state of the ego vehicle and publishes predicted objects; its components are detection, tracking, and prediction (Sect.~\ref{sec:background}). The pipeline is executed in open loop on recorded drives of a public benchmark data set, which makes every run repeatable. \todo{Name the data set, the number of scenes, and the Autoware version once the replay set-up is fixed.} The monitors synthesised from the contracts of Sect.~\ref{sec:patterns} run as compiled Lean code behind an adapter that samples the observed topics once per interval.

To answer RQ1, we classify safety requirements on perception pipelines by whether they can be expressed in the contract language. Since requirements collected from the literature and from the documentation of Autoware informed the design of the language, they cannot serve as a measurement. We therefore distinguish a \emph{design set}, which was available while the language was developed, from a \emph{held-out set} compiled from independent sources after the language had been frozen, and report both. A requirement is rated \emph{expressible} if it is an instance of a pattern of Table~\ref{tab:patterns}, \emph{expressible with a signal} if it needs an atom over a value computed outside the monitor, \emph{partially expressible} if only a weakened form can be stated, and \emph{not expressible} otherwise.

To answer RQ2 and RQ3, we inject faults into the recorded runs at the level of topics. The fault model comprises six classes: loss of messages, delay, frozen output, values out of range, a drop in confidence, and missing or additional objects. Each fault is injected either at the input of a component, where it constitutes a violation of the component's assumption by its environment, or at its output, where it constitutes a violation of its guarantee. The place of injection is the ground truth for RQ3. For RQ2, we measure for each injected fault whether a component monitor raises an alarm, the number of intervals between injection and alarm, and the number of alarms in runs without injected faults. For RQ3, we measure the share of injected faults that the assumption and guarantee monitors attribute to the party at which the fault was injected.
